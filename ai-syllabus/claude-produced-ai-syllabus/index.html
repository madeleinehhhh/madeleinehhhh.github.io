AI & Machine Learning: A Self-Directed Syllabus for the Analytically Minded Developer

Created by Claude.

Tailored for backgrounds in analytic philosophy and web development

This syllabus is organized into five modules that build on each other, moving from intellectual history and conceptual foundations through to hands-on development and frontier research questions. Each module can take 2–6 weeks depending on depth of engagement. Resources are curated for someone who thinks carefully about concepts and can read technical material, but who is approaching ML from the outside.

Module 1: Intellectual History & Conceptual Foundations

Before touching any code, it pays to understand the long arc of AI research — the debates, the dead ends, and why the current moment looks the way it does. Your philosophy background gives you a real advantage here.

Core Reading

Machines Who Think — Pamela McCorduck
Book
Definitive intellectual history of AI from antiquity to the 1980s. Readable narrative history.
Alchemy and AI — Hubert Dreyfus (1965)
Essay
Philosophy
The foundational philosophical critique of early symbolic AI. Deeply relevant to philosophy of mind debates.
What Computers Can't Do — Hubert Dreyfus
Book
Philosophy
Dreyfus's full case against symbolic AI and the rationalist tradition. Read alongside the rebuttal literature.
A Proposal for the Dartmouth Summer Research Project on AI (1955)
Article
The founding document of AI as a field. Short, historically revealing.
Computing Machinery and Intelligence — Alan Turing (1950)
Essay
The original Turing Test paper. Still philosophically rich and surprisingly readable.
Minds, Brains, and Programs — John Searle (1980)
Article
Philosophy
The Chinese Room argument. Essential reading given current debates about LLM understanding.

Supplemental

Lex Fridman #302 — Yann LeCun
Podcast
LeCun's skepticism about current architectures. Good intro to live debates about what's missing.
The Bitter Lesson — Rich Sutton (2019)
Essay
Influential short essay arguing that general methods beat human-engineered solutions. Sparks real debate.

Module 2: How Neural Networks Actually Work

Build genuine intuition for what is happening inside these models before diving into theory. The goal is geometric and mechanical understanding, not just metaphor.

But what is a neural network? — 3Blue1Brown
Video
The best visual introduction to neural nets. Watch all 4 episodes in the series.
Neural Networks: Zero to Hero — Andrej Karpathy
Video
Builds a GPT from scratch in Python. Exceptional explanations. Do the coding exercises.
Transformers from Scratch — Andrej Karpathy (makemore series)
Video
Technical
Continuation of Zero to Hero, focuses on attention mechanisms.
The Illustrated Transformer — Jay Alammar
Blog
The clearest visual walkthrough of the transformer architecture. Essential companion to the original paper.
The Illustrated GPT-2 — Jay Alammar
Blog
Extends the transformer post to autoregressive language models. Concrete and well-illustrated.
Lilian Weng's Blog
Blog
Technical
Research scientist at OpenAI; deep technical posts on attention, RL, diffusion. High-quality reference.
Attention Is All You Need (2017)
Paper
The transformer paper. Dense, but worth reading once you have the illustrated versions as scaffolding.
Deep Learning — Goodfellow, Bengio, Courville
Book
Free
The standard graduate textbook. Use as reference rather than reading cover to cover.
Hands-On Machine Learning — Aurélien Géron
Book
Practical, code-first introduction. Great bridge between intuition and implementation.

Module 3: The LLM Revolution — From GPT to Now

Understand the specific developments that led to the current generation of large language models — scaling laws, RLHF, and the emergence of surprising capabilities.

Key Papers (Read in Order)

Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models (2020)
Paper
The paper that made scaling compute and data the dominant research strategy. Philosophically interesting on emergence.
Language Models are Few-Shot Learners / GPT-3 (2020)
Paper
The GPT-3 paper. Introduced in-context learning. Read the introduction and results sections at minimum.
Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback (InstructGPT)
Paper
How RLHF works. The technique behind ChatGPT. Readable and important.
Sparks of AGI — Microsoft Research (2023)
Paper
Controversial but worth reading as a document of the current moment. Raises real philosophical questions.

Articles, Blogs & Podcasts

What Is ChatGPT Doing and Why Does It Work? — Stephen Wolfram
Blog
Long but accessible. Good for conceptual understanding without heavy math.
Stochastic Parrots — Bender et al. (2021)
Article
Philosophy
Influential critical perspective on LLMs. Raises questions about meaning and statistical correlation.
The AI Hype Cycle Is Distracting Us — Gary Marcus
Blog
Marcus's ongoing skeptical commentary. Good counterweight to enthusiasm. Follow his Substack.
Import AI — Jack Clark
Newsletter
Weekly newsletter by co-founder of Anthropic. Tracks research developments with good editorial judgment.
80,000 Hours — Interview with Paul Christiano
Podcast
Alignment researcher on what the hard problems actually are. Rigorous and honest.
MLST — Various Episodes
Podcast
Technical
Technical podcast with researchers. Higher level than Lex Fridman, good once you have foundations.

Module 5: Frontier Questions & Philosophy of AI

This is where your analytic philosophy background is most valuable. The open questions in alignment, interpretability, and AI ethics are genuinely hard philosophical problems — not merely engineering challenges.

Alignment & Safety

AGI Safety from First Principles — Richard Ngo
Essay
The clearest conceptual overview of why alignment is hard. Philosophically rigorous.
AI Safety — Paul Christiano
Blog
Blog by one of the leading alignment researchers. Dense but worth it.
Concrete Problems in AI Safety — Amodei et al. (2016)
Paper
Lays out specific technical problems in making AI systems behave as intended.
Human Compatible — Stuart Russell
Book
Best book-length treatment of the alignment problem. Clear philosophical argument.

Interpretability

Transformer Circuits Thread — Anthropic
Blog
Technical
Anthropic's ongoing research into mechanistic interpretability. Technically demanding but fascinating.
Zoom In: An Introduction to Circuits — Distill.pub
Article
Distill.pub has the best visual explanations of neural network internals. The whole site is worth exploring.
Chris Olah's Blog
Blog
Olah pioneered interpretability research. His older posts on neural net visualization are foundational.

Philosophy of Mind

Language Models and Linguistic Knowledge — Linzen (2019)
Paper
Philosophy
Asks whether LLMs know grammar or merely predict it. Connects to debates about competence vs. performance.
The Language Instinct — Steven Pinker
Book
Background on linguistics and cognitive science. Useful context for evaluating LLM language claims.
On the Measure of Intelligence — François Chollet (2019)
Essay
Philosophy
Proposes a rigorous definition of intelligence and critiques benchmark-based evaluation. Philosophically sharp.
The Alignment Forum
Newsletter
Community of alignment researchers. High signal-to-noise ratio. Read the featured posts first.

Ongoing Sources

Simon Willison's Blog
Blog
Developer perspective on AI tools. Practical, honest, well-curated. Excellent for staying current.
The Batch — Andrew Ng
Newsletter
Weekly AI newsletter with good editorial perspective. Good balance of technical and strategic.
Anthropic Research Blog
Blog
Primary source on interpretability, alignment, and model evaluations from Anthropic.
arXiv cs.AI and cs.LG sections
Paper
Preprint server where all major ML research appears first. Follow to track the frontier.

A Note on Sequencing

You don't need to finish Module 1 before starting Module 4. A good approach is to run conceptual reading (Modules 1–2) alongside practical building (Module 4) — each will make the other more meaningful. Module 3 fills in the historical arc of recent developments and is best read once you have enough technical vocabulary from Module 2 to evaluate the claims being made.

Module 5 can begin anytime, but will hit differently once you've built something and confronted firsthand how these systems behave in ways that are hard to predict or explain.

This syllabus was compiled February 2026. The field moves fast — check publication dates and supplement with recent work from arXiv and the blogs listed in Module 5.
